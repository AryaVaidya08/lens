## Basics
Biofreeze is an over-the-counter topical analgesic whose active ingredient is
menthol. It is used for temporary relief of minor aches and pains of muscles and
joints, including simple backache, arthritis, strains, bruises, and sprains. It
works as a counterirritant rather than by treating the underlying condition.
- Over-the-counter menthol gel, roll-on, and spray
- Temporary relief of minor muscle and joint pain
- Counterirritant: it does not treat the underlying condition

## Dosing
Adults and children 12 years and older apply a thin layer to the affected area
no more than four times daily. Wash hands after application unless the hands are
the treatment site. Do not use in children under 12 without clinician advice.
- Apply to the affected area up to 4 times daily
- Ages 12 and older; ask a clinician for younger patients
- Wash hands after applying unless treating the hands

## Side effects
Adverse effects are almost entirely local: redness, transient burning or stinging,
and skin irritation at the application site. Serious reactions are rare but have
been reported as blistering or chemical burns, usually after tight bandaging or
applied heat. Systemic adverse effects are not expected at labeled doses.
- Local redness, burning, stinging, and irritation are the usual effects
- Rare blistering or chemical burns, usually with heat or occlusion
- Stop use if severe irritation or blistering develops

## Warnings
For external use only. Keep away from the eyes and mucous membranes, and do not
apply to wounded, broken, or irritated skin. Do not bandage tightly or apply heat,
since both increase absorption and the risk of burns. Stop use and see a clinician
if the condition worsens, if symptoms last more than seven days, or if they clear
up and then recur within a few days.
- External use only; avoid eyes, mucous membranes, and broken skin
- Do not combine with heating pads or tight bandages
- Reassess if pain lasts more than 7 days or worsens

## Interactions
There are no clinically meaningful systemic drug interactions at labeled topical
doses, because systemic absorption of menthol is low. The practical concern is
layering: applying other topical analgesics such as capsaicin, salicylates, or
lidocaine to the same site increases local irritation without added benefit.
- No meaningful systemic interactions at labeled doses
- Avoid layering with other topical analgesics on the same site
- Local irritation, not systemic toxicity, is the limiting factor

## Trial data
Randomized comparisons of topical menthol against placebo gel in delayed-onset
muscle soreness reported modest short-term reductions in pain scores, with the
effect concentrated in the first hour after application. Evidence in chronic
musculoskeletal pain is weaker and mostly short duration, so it is best positioned
as adjunctive symptomatic relief rather than a primary analgesic strategy.
- Modest short-term pain reduction versus placebo, strongest in the first hour
- Chronic pain evidence is limited and short duration
- Best positioned as adjunctive relief alongside a primary plan
